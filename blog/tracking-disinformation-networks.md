Over six months, we mapped a network of 340 websites and 1,200
social media accounts coordinating inauthentic behaviour across
seven European countries. What we found was not a single operation
but an ecosystem — loosely affiliated, opportunistic, and adaptive.

## Methodology

We started with a seed list of 40 known disinformation outlets
identified by EU DisinfoLab. From there, we used three signals:

- Shared hosting infrastructure (ASN and IP clustering)
- Cross-posting patterns (same content, near-simultaneous)
- Backlink networks (mutual citation without editorial relation)

The graph grew to 1,200 nodes before we applied community detection
to identify clusters.

## What We Found

Five distinct clusters emerged, each targeting different audiences:

- A Russophone cluster focused on Baltic states
- A domestic-targeting cluster in France and Germany
- A pan-European climate denial network
- Two smaller clusters linked to far-right party infrastructure

The most sophisticated cluster used a relay structure: content
originated in one jurisdiction, translated by automated tools,
and distributed through accounts in another — making attribution
deliberately difficult.

## The Limits of This Work

Computational methods identify patterns. They do not establish intent.
We spent as much time on legal review as on data analysis, ensuring
every claim was grounded in observable behaviour rather than inference.
